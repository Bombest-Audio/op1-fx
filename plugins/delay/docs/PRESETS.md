# Delay factory presets

Generated by `delay_convert.py` from real-world targets. Tempo-synced entries assume 90 BPM (quarter 667 ms, 1/8 333 ms, 1/16 167 ms) and say 90 in the name. Every preset sets all four parameters (`time`, `speed`, `fdb`, `input`); `act` is never stored. `presets.json` serves both the MPC VST2 port and the JUCE plugin.

Real units: Delay is the effective echo time (loop length divided by tape speed). Tape speed is the SPEED ratio after the 8 s ceiling. Tone is the approximate -3 dB corner of one pass through the loop low-pass at 44.1 kHz; each repeat passes it again, so repeats darken further. FDB is the linear loop gain (and dB). INPUT is the linear send gain into the tape (and dB); the dry signal always stays at unity.

## Conversion notes

- TIME and SPEED interact. The delay is `base(TIME) / rate(SPEED)`, and SPEED alone also sets the echo tone. A naive inverse that maps the wanted delay straight to TIME (SPEED at full) would be wrong whenever SPEED is below 1. The converter picks a tape speed for the tone first, then sets `base = delay * rate`, `TIME = log2(base * 32) / 5`, `SPEED = rate`. It asserts base is between 31.25 ms and 1 s, rate is at least 1/32 and at least base/8 (the 8 s ceiling), then forward-maps every result and checks delay, rate and input gain against the targets.
- FDB is the gain itself, so it needs no conversion. INPUT is `10^(dB/20)`.
- At rates above 0.72 the loop poles hit the 0.45 x sample rate cap (19.8 kHz at 44.1 kHz), so Slap 16th and Drum Bus are capped there. The tone column accounts for that at 44.1 kHz; on a 96 kHz JUCE host the corners for those two sit higher.
- The core has one set of controls for both channels, no high-pass, and no separate left and right times. Devvon's dual-delay trick, the 300 Hz low cut of the Abbey Road throw and ping pong cannot be set in these presets.
- Devvon gives no direct guidance for kick, snare, 808 or drum bus delays. Those entries say so in their source column.
